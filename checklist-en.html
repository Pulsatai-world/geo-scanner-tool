Why the rules are published

Generative engine optimisation is one machine reading another. A verdict about a page should therefore be reproducible by anyone holding the same page — so every check below states the exact rule that decides it.

Three things follow. Two scans of an unchanged site return the same result, which is what makes progress between audits measurable rather than anecdotal. Nothing rests on the judgement of whoever happened to run the scan. And where a factor genuinely cannot be settled remotely it is reported as unverified and excluded from the score entirely.

This document is generated directly from the scanner own check registry. It cannot describe a check the tool does not run, or omit one it does.

Layer 1 — Access

Can AI systems reach this site at all?
Checks11 Scoringreported separately

Whether crawlers and retrieval bots can fetch the site, and whether anything blocks, redirects or rate-limits them. Nothing downstream matters if this fails.

Who can act on it: Hosting, DNS and CDN — usually the host or platform, not the web team.

1 Site reachability from the scanner site-reachability not scored
What is measured
Whether any request to the origin completes, and the transport-level reason when none do.
Rule
Reported as UNVERIFIED whenever every user-agent fails at the connection layer. Never a failure of the site: a refused connection is most often edge protection rejecting the scanner's network, and the site typically serves real visitors normally throughout.
Why it matters for citation
Distinguishes "we could not measure this" from "this site is broken". Scoring an unreachable site would publish our network conditions as a verdict about the client.
2 www / non-www resolution host-variant
What is measured
Whether both the apex domain and the www hostname resolve and serve the site.
Rule
FAIL when one form resolves and the other does not. The scan continues against whichever answers.
Why it matters for citation
Anyone typing or linking the missing form reaches nothing, and engines see two hostnames rather than one consolidated site.
3 Multi-user-agent crawl test multi-ua
What is measured
The homepage fetched with seven user-agents: a browser, GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Googlebot, OAI-SearchBot, PerplexityBot and a plain default.
Rule
FAIL only when the server returns an explicit refusal (401, 402, 403, 405, 451) to a bot while the browser request succeeds. Rate limiting (429) and dropped connections WARN instead — they are transient, and scanning can cause them.
Why it matters for citation
Catches user-agent-based bot blocking at the host. Requires a deliberate refusal status: a timeout is evidence of an unreliable connection, not of a policy.
4 robots.txt AI crawler rules robots-txt
What is measured
18 crawler tokens evaluated against robots.txt, split into live retrieval, model training and search indexing.
Rule
FAIL if any retrieval or search crawler is blocked (OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Claude-User, Claude-SearchBot, PerplexityBot, Perplexity-User, Google-Extended, Googlebot, Bingbot, *). WARNING if only training crawlers are blocked (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot, Applebot-Extended and similar).
Why it matters for citation
Blocking retrieval means the site cannot be cited in answers at all. Blocking training is a legitimate licensing choice with a slower cost, so it is named rather than treated as a defect. Google-Extended is singled out because the one-click AI toggle in Yoast and RankMath sets it, removing sites from Gemini without their owners knowing.
5 X-Robots-Tag header x-robots-tag
What is measured
The X-Robots-Tag HTTP response header.
Rule
FAIL if it contains noindex or none. WARNING if present without those directives.
Why it matters for citation
A server-level indexing block set in .htaccess, nginx config or a plugin — invisible in the page source, and easily missed by hand.
6 Meta robots noindex tag noindex-meta
What is measured
meta name="robots" and meta name="googlebot" in the document head.
Rule
FAIL if either contains noindex.
Why it matters for citation
Commonly left on after a staging launch, or by the WordPress "discourage search engines" setting.
7 XML sitemap sitemap
What is measured
Sitemap declarations in robots.txt, then five common paths: /sitemap.xml, /sitemap_index.xml, /wp-sitemap.xml, /sitemap-index.xml, /sitemap1.xml.
Rule
PASS on a valid sitemap with 3 or more URLs. WARNING if none is found, or fewer than 3 URLs are listed. FAIL if a sitemap exists but is empty or malformed.
Why it matters for citation
Speeds discovery for every crawler. Checking declarations first avoids reporting a correctly configured site as having no sitemap.
8 Response time response-time
What is measured
The fastest of two isolated homepage requests, each issued with nothing else contending for the origin.
Rule
WARNING above 2500ms. Never a FAIL — a timing sample should not be able to gate anything. UNVERIFIED if no request completed.
Why it matters for citation
Slow origins get abandoned by crawlers. Two isolated samples rather than one keeps the figure stable enough to compare across re-audits.
9 Edge bot protection (CDN/WAF) edge-protection not scored
What is measured
CDN and WAF fingerprints in response headers — Cloudflare, Sucuri, Akamai, Fastly, Imperva.
Rule
Always UNVERIFIED when a provider is detected. Edge services identify verified bots by source IP range, not user-agent string, so user-agent testing cannot settle whether AI crawlers are permitted — it passes regardless.
Why it matters for citation
The scanner's clearest blind spot, reported as such rather than hidden behind a pass. Carries the exact manual check, and names the hosting platform when the CDN belongs to the platform rather than the client.
10 Hosting platform hosting-platform not scored
What is measured
Platform fingerprints in the page and headers — Lovable, Wix, Squarespace, Framer, Webflow, Shopify, self-hosted WordPress.
Rule
Informational. Never a pass or a failure.
Why it matters for citation
Decides who can fix what. On a managed platform the CDN belongs to the host, so advice to change bot settings sends the client looking for a dashboard they do not have.
11 llms.txt llms-txt not scored
What is measured
Presence of /llms.txt.
Rule
Presence only. Its absence is never counted against a site.
Why it matters for citation
An emerging convention that no major engine has committed to honouring. Reported as an early-mover signal, not a requirement.

Layer 2 — Machine readability

Can a machine parse the page and understand its structure?
Checks15 Scoringcounts toward score

Whether the content exists in the HTML without JavaScript, and whether titles, metadata, structured data, headings and landmarks describe it in a form a machine can interpret.

Who can act on it: Developer or CMS.

1 Content visible without JavaScript js-rendering
What is measured
Readable words in the server response, script count, and whether a framework mount point arrived empty.
Rule
FAIL if the server returns under 60 words alongside scripts, or a mount point (#root, #__next, #app) contains under 30 words.
Why it matters for citation
Most AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript. If this fails, every other on-page result for the page is unreliable — the checks are reading an empty shell, not the real page.
2 Title tag title
What is measured
Presence and length of the title element.
Rule
FAIL if missing. WARNING above 60 characters.
Why it matters for citation
The strongest single statement of what a page is about, used by both search and AI surfaces.
3 Meta description meta-description
What is measured
Presence and length of the meta description.
Rule
FAIL if missing. WARNING above 160 characters.
Why it matters for citation
Frequently lifted verbatim as the summary shown alongside a citation.
4 Schema.org / JSON-LD schema
What is measured
JSON-LD blocks parsed for @type, compared against eight common types.
Rule
FAIL if no structured data is present. WARNING if more than four common types are absent.
Why it matters for citation
The most direct way to state machine-readable facts about an entity. Among the highest-leverage signals available for citation.
5 Heading structure headings
What is measured
Count of h1 elements and whether heading levels are skipped.
Rule
PASS with exactly one h1 and no skipped levels. FAIL with no h1. WARNING otherwise.
Why it matters for citation
Headings are how a machine segments a page into passages it can retrieve individually.
6 Sequential heading hierarchy heading-hierarchy
What is measured
Whether heading levels descend without gaps.
Rule
WARNING when a level is skipped (h1 straight to h3).
Why it matters for citation
A broken hierarchy produces a misleading document outline, blurring where one topic ends and the next begins.
7 Canonical tag canonical
What is measured
link rel="canonical" and whether it is self-referencing or cross-domain.
Rule
FAIL if it points to another domain. WARNING if absent.
Why it matters for citation
A canonical left pointing at staging after a migration can suppress the live page entirely.
8 Open Graph tags open-graph
What is measured
og:title, og:description and og:type.
Rule
FAIL if all three are missing. WARNING if some are.
Why it matters for citation
Controls how the page is represented when shared or previewed, including by tools that fetch preview metadata.
9 Image alt text coverage image-alt
What is measured
Proportion of img elements carrying non-empty alt text.
Rule
PASS at 80% or above. WARNING from 40%. FAIL below 40%.
Why it matters for citation
Alt text is the only description of an image a non-visual system receives.
10 Main landmark presence main-landmark
What is measured
A main element or role="main".
Rule
FAIL if absent.
Why it matters for citation
Tells an agent which part of the document is the content, as distinct from navigation and chrome.
11 Form input labels form-labels
What is measured
Whether form inputs have associated labels or accessible names.
Rule
WARNING when inputs lack them.
Why it matters for citation
An AI agent completing a form on a user's behalf can only identify a field by its accessible name.
12 Accessibility tree health a11y-tree-health
What is measured
Composite of the landmark, heading hierarchy, form label and image alt results.
Rule
Derived from its component checks.
Why it matters for citation
The accessibility tree is broadly what an agentic browser navigates by, so its health predicts how well an agent can operate the page.
13 Contact info machine-readability contact-machine-readable
What is measured
tel: and mailto: links, and ContactPoint schema. Only applied when the page shows contact details at all.
Rule
WARNING when contact details appear as plain text only.
Why it matters for citation
Lets an agent act on the details rather than merely read them.
14 Structured data completeness schema-completeness
What is measured
Every property actually present on each recognised schema.org type, checked against that type’s required and recommended properties.
Rule
FAIL when a type is missing a required property (Organization without name or url, LocalBusiness without an address). WARNING when most recommended properties are absent. Only recognised types are assessed.
Why it matters for citation
Presence alone proves nothing. A block carrying only @type and a name gives an engine no way to tie the markup to a real entity, yet it passes a presence check exactly as a complete one does.
15 FAQ schema vs visible content faq-schema-match
What is measured
FAQPage schema questions matched against text visible on the page. Only applied where FAQ schema exists.
Rule
PASS when 70% or more of schema questions appear in the visible content.
Why it matters for citation
Structured data that does not reflect the page can be treated as manipulative.

Layer 3 — Substance & authority

Is there anything here worth citing, and any reason to trust it?
Checks9 Scoringcounts toward score

Whether the page carries enough specific, well-scoped content to be quoted, and whether it demonstrates a real, accountable organisation behind it. A page can pass every technical check and still never be cited because there is nothing in it to quote.

Who can act on it: Content and marketing.

1 Content depth for citation content-depth
What is measured
Words of main content, with navigation, header and footer removed.
Rule
FAIL under 300 words. WARNING under 600. PASS at 600 or above; 1200 or above is recorded as substantial.
Why it matters for citation
Below roughly 300 words there is not enough for an engine to extract a useful passage. A page can be crawled perfectly and never be quoted because there is nothing in it worth quoting.
2 Page scope — should this be split? page-scope
What is measured
Content split at h2 boundaries and measured per section, plus whether navigation is built entirely from in-page anchors.
Rule
FAIL when navigation is anchor-only across 3 or more topics — a single-page site. WARNING at 4 or more sections averaging under 120 words.
Why it matters for citation
Engines retrieve and cite at page level. Topics sharing one document cannot be returned for queries about them individually, however good the writing.
3 Authority & credibility signals authority-signals
What is measured
Six signals, detected bilingually (English and Spanish): named people, client evidence, credentials, physical address, direct contact links, external profile links including sameAs.
Rule
PASS at 5 of 6 or more. WARNING at 3 or 4. FAIL at 2 or fewer.
Why it matters for citation
Engines favour sources visibly belonging to a real, accountable organisation. Without these a site reads as anonymous marketing copy however well it is built.
4 Answer-shaped content answer-format
What is measured
Question-form headings (English and Spanish), list items, tables and definition lists.
Rule
PASS with 2 or more question-form headings, or a structure score of 12 or more (lists count 1, tables 5, definition lists 3).
Why it matters for citation
Retrieval matches a question to a passage that answers it. Q&A sections, lists and comparison tables are markedly easier to lift an answer from than continuous prose.
5 Named entity / specificity signal entities
What is measured
Proper-noun phrases, numbers and year references in the main content.
Rule
WARNING when fewer than 3 proper nouns and fewer than 3 numbers are present.
Why it matters for citation
Specific, checkable facts are what gets quoted. Generic marketing language offers nothing to cite.
6 First-250-words specificity first-250-specificity
What is measured
The same entity heuristic applied to the opening 250 words only.
Rule
PASS with 3 or more proper nouns, or 3 or more numbers, in the opening.
Why it matters for citation
Extraction weights the beginning of a page more heavily than material further down.
7 Author / credential attribution author-attribution
What is measured
Bylines, rel="author" links, and Person schema tied to authorship.
Rule
WARNING when none is found.
Why it matters for citation
Named, credentialed sources are weighted more heavily than unattributed corporate copy.
8 Freshness signals content-freshness
What is measured
dateModified, datePublished and uploadDate in structured data, <time datetime> elements, and article date metadata.
Rule
WARNING when no machine-readable date exists at all, or when the most recent is over a year old. PASS within a year.
Why it matters for citation
Engines weight recency. A page carrying no readable date cannot be assessed for it, which is itself a disadvantage against a competitor whose page is visibly current.
9 Boilerplate / templated content boilerplate
What is measured
Pairwise five-word shingle similarity between analysed pages.
Rule
WARNING above 60% similarity between any pair. UNVERIFIED with fewer than two pages, since there is nothing to compare.
Why it matters for citation
Near-identical pages compete with each other and signal low-value templating.
